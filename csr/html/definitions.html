2. Definitions & Historical Context

Origins, Key Concepts, and the Triple Bottom Line

Historical Evolution

Modern CSR originated in the United States of America[cite: 1]. It was first formally introduced by Howard R. Bowen in his 1953 publication, "Social Responsibilities of the Businessman"[cite: 1].

Initially, CSR received significantly more attention in the US than in European nations[cite: 1]. This disparity stems from the difference in economic systems: in a free market economy like the US, corporate social responsibility carries greater weight compared to a social market economy (such as Germany), where the state traditionally fulfills strong social welfare functions[cite: 1].

From the 1970s onward, public demand for operational transparency, product safety, and environmental protection intensified[cite: 1]. Emerging phenomena, such as standardized labor regulations and global ecological risks, firmly linked sustainability with CSR[cite: 1].

Key Milestone

1953

Howard R. Bowen publishes "Social Responsibilities of the Businessman"[cite: 1]

Core Definition & European Perspective

In simple terms, CSR describes a company's responsibility toward society, encompassing voluntary economic activities aimed at sustainability[cite: 1].

"[CSR is] a concept whereby companies integrate social and environmental concerns in their business operations and in their interaction with their stakeholders on a voluntary basis."

European Commission Green Paper (July 2001) "Promoting a European framework for Corporate Social Responsibility"[cite: 1]

The Three-Pillar Model (Triple Bottom Line)

Sustainability in CSR is fundamentally anchored on the Three-Pillar Model (Triple Bottom Line), ensuring that current and future needs are met through balanced economic, ecological, and social practices[cite: 1].

Figure 1: The Triple-Bottom-Line Model (Economy, Ecology, Social)[cite: 1]
1. Economy (Ökonomie)

Ensuring long-term financial viability, ethical competitiveness, and value creation without exploiting resources[cite: 1].

2. Ecology (Ökologie)

Minimizing environmental footprints, resource consumption, and emissions to preserve natural habitats[cite: 1].

3. Social (Soziales)

Fostering fair working conditions, human rights, community development, and employee well-being[cite: 1].
