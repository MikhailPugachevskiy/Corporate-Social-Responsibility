4. Real-World Case Studies

Practical Implementation in SMEs and Corporate Crises (Volkswagen)

4.1 Small and Medium Enterprises (KMU / SMEs)

Informal & Regionally Anchored Responsibility

SME Practice

Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs / KMU) often practice CSR naturally without formalizing it into large marketing campaigns or complex sustainability reports[cite: 1]. Their engagement is rooted in personal values, local community presence, and direct relationships with employees and local stakeholders[cite: 1].

Key Characteristics:
  • High level of personal owner/manager commitment[cite: 1].
  • Strong focus on regional community sponsorship[cite: 1].
  • Direct, flexible workplace improvements and employee support[cite: 1].
Challenges:
  • Limited financial and human resources[cite: 1].
  • Lack of formal metrics and communication strategies[cite: 1].

4.2 Corporate Case Study: Volkswagen AG (VW)

The Emissions Scandal ("Dieselgate")

Critical Case

Volkswagen had long established elaborate CSR programs and sustained a positive corporate image[cite: 1]. However, the 2015 emissions scandal revealed a stark contradiction between official CSR statements and actual corporate behavior[cite: 1].

The Conflict

To achieve aggressive growth goals in the US market, defeat devices were installed in diesel engines to bypass environmental regulations[cite: 1]. Profitability and short-term market share were prioritized over legal and ethical compliance[cite: 1].

Consequences & Realignment

Massive financial penalties, severe loss of trust, and a complete restructuring of governance[cite: 1]. VW subsequently accelerated its strategic shift toward electromobility and transparent sustainability reporting[cite: 1].

Takeaway: CSR cannot exist merely as a public relations tool. Without genuine alignment between top-level strategy, executive incentives, and ethical standards, public credibility can be destroyed instantly[cite: 1].
